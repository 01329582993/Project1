The Motivation
Why Traditional Routing Fails in FANETs
High UAV velocity, rapid link decay, and energy constraints break classical protocols.
❌ Greedy Geographic
  • Forwards strictly to the neighbor closest to the destination.
  • Suffers from routing voids (local minimum traps).
  • High retransmissions and MAC contention under mobility.
⚠️ Classical Q-Routing
  • Learns next-hop based on delay estimates.
  • Blind exploration: Explores unreliable links that cause severe latency spikes.
  • Ignores battery and transmission power consumption.
💡 What FANETs Need
  • Multi-Objective: Jointly balance PDR, Delay, and Power.
  • Risk-Bounded Exploration: Never explore paths likely to violate latency deadlines.
  • Cooperative Knowledge: Learn from neighbors without central infrastructure.

Architecture Overview
The Three Architectural Pillars of RLFR
A decentralized reinforcement learning framework tailored for time-critical aerial swarms.
1️⃣ Multi-Objective Utility

Joint Reward Function

  • Rewards delivery success ($\kappa$).
  • Penalizes end-to-end latency ($\tau$).
  • Penalizes transmission energy ($w$).
  • Trades off speed vs. drone battery life.
2️⃣ Safe Boltzmann Exploration

Risk-Constrained Action Selection

  • Maintains a separate Risk Table $R(s,a)$.
  • Tracks the probability of exceeding latency deadline $\mu=40\text{ ms}$.
  • Penalizes risky paths in the softmax selection.
3️⃣ Shared Experience

Distributed Bellman Updates

  • Nodes broadcast local state value $V(s)$ in periodic HELLO beacons.
  • Neighbors factor in shared value $\upsilon \sum V_j$.
  • Accelerates learning convergence in dynamic meshes.

Mathematical Formulation
1. Multi-Objective Utility Function
Formulating the reinforcement learning reward to balance three competing objectives.
Equation 3 in Paper
u⁽ᵏ⁾ = κ − c₁·τ − c₂·w
  • κ ∈ {0, 1}: Delivery Success Indicator (1 if destination ACK received, 0 otherwise).
  • τ: Normalized E2E Latency ($\tau = \Delta t / T_{\max}$).
  • w: Transmission Energy ($w = p \cdot \frac{z}{r}$ where $p$ is transmit power, $z$ is packet size, $r$ is transmission rate).
  • c₁ = 0.8, c₂ = 0.6: Hyperparameter weighting coefficients.
⚡ Transmission Power Control

Rather than transmitting at a fixed maximum power, the action space selects both the next-hop UAV and the power level:

a = (next_hop, p),   p ∈ {25, 50, 75, 100} mW
  • Reduces electromagnetic interference and collisions.
  • Conserves battery on long-duration aerial missions.
  • Higher power is selected dynamically only when channel SNR $\xi$ is low.
🎙️ Presenter Script (Say this):
"Here is the exact utility formula: u equals kappa minus c1 times tau minus c2 times w. Kappa is 1 if the packet reaches its final destination. Tau is the packet's latency, and w is the energy expended to send it, computed as power times transmission duration. Notice that our action chooses both the next-hop relay drone and the transmit power level between 25 and 100 milliwatts. This ensures the drone only spends the power necessary to maintain link quality."
Mathematical Formulation
2. Safe Exploration & Latency-Risk Constraint
Guiding exploration using a dual Q-value and Risk-value Boltzmann distribution.
Modified Boltzmann Policy (Eq. 2)
π(s, a) = exp(Q(s,a) − c·R(s,a)) / Σ exp(...)
  • Standard Boltzmann uses only $\exp(Q(s, a))$.
  • RLFR introduces the latency penalty c · R(s, a) ($c = 0.5$).
  • Actions that frequently miss latency deadlines have high $R(s, a)$, drastically reducing their forwarding probability.
Risk Table Update Rule (Eq. 5 & 6)
l⁽ᵏ⁾ = 𝟙(τ > μ) (μ = 40 ms)
R(s,a) ← (1 − β)·R(s,a) + β·l⁽ᵏ⁾
  • l⁽ᵏ⁾ is a binary indicator: 1 if latency exceeds $40\text{ ms}$, 0 otherwise.
  • β = 0.8 provides rapid exponential smoothing.
  • Allows immediate reaction when a link becomes congested or unstable.
🎙️ Presenter Script (Say this):
"This slide shows why RLFR is called a 'safe' reinforcement learning protocol. In traditional Q-learning, the agent explores actions randomly to discover new paths. But in FANETs, picking a bad link could drop video streams or crash emergency telemetry. In RLFR, we keep a separate Risk Table, R(s, a), which tracks whether latency exceeded the 40 millisecond threshold. In the Boltzmann policy, the risk term acts as a penalty in the exponent. As a result, risky paths are avoided while still permitting safe exploration."
Mathematical Formulation
3. Distributed Bellman Update with Shared Experience
Accelerating distributed Q-learning convergence without centralized coordination.
Distributed Bellman Equation (Eq. 4)
Q(s,a) ← (1 − α)·Q(s,a) + α·[ u + λ·max Q(s′,â) + υ·Σ Vⱼ(sⱼ) ]
Beacon Value Piggybacking
  • Each UAV $j$ computes its state-value function:
    Vⱼ(sⱼ) = max_â Q(sⱼ, â)
  • This float value is embedded inside periodic RLFRHelloPacket broadcasts (every $0.5\text{ s}$).
  • No extra control overhead or dedicated message exchanges needed.
Loop Avoidance Cache (Ω)
  • In multi-hop aerial meshes, packets can loop back if routing tables fluctuate.
  • Node tracks recent (source_id, packet_id) pairs in cache $\Omega$.
  • Duplicate packets are instantly dropped, eliminating broadcast storms and routing cycles.
🎙️ Presenter Script (Say this):
"Here is the core Bellman update. In addition to the standard reward u and discounted future Q-value, look at the cooperative term: upsilon times the sum of V_j. Every drone calculates its local value function—the maximum Q-value for its state—and inserts it into its regular 0.5-second HELLO beacon. Neighbors receive this value and incorporate it into their learning. This allows the entire swarm to share experience cooperatively without needing a centralized controller."
Code Implementation
How RLFR is Implemented in UavNetSim
Modular, clean, and fully integrated with UavNetSim's discrete-event simulation engine.
Package: routing/rlfr/
  • rlfr_packet.py:
    • RLFRHelloPacket: Transmits coordinates, velocity, battery, and $V(s)$.
    • RLFRAckPacket: Reports delivery ACK, E2E delay $\tau$, and SNR $\xi$.
  • rlfr_table.py:
    • Implements Q-table, Risk-table $R(s,a)$, Boltzmann selection, and loop cache $\Omega$.
  • rlfr.py:
    • Protocol controller: Packet queuing, MAC layer interface, overhearing, and Bellman update triggers.
System Integration Points
  • entities/drone.py: Registered "RLFR": RLFR in protocol dispatch map.
  • routing/parameters.py: Configured paper hyperparameters ($\alpha=0.7, \lambda=0.9, c=0.5, \beta=0.8, \mu=40\text{ ms}$).
  • main.py CLI: Run via --routing RLFR.
  • Web UI (api/app.py): Appears in dropdown at http://127.0.0.1:8000.
